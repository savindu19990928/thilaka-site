# Thilaka Ayurveda — Dynamic Website & Product Catalogue Specification

> **Project purpose:** Build a modern, responsive website for Thilaka Ayurveda with a product catalogue, business information and contact options. The website must be manageable through a secure backend/admin dashboard so authorized staff can update products, categories, images, pages, opening hours, contact details, banners and other website content without changing code.
>
> **Critical business rule — catalogue only, NOT online selling:** Customers may browse the product catalogue and contact the shop to ask questions or check availability. **To purchase a product, the customer must physically visit the Thilaka Ayurveda shop.** The website must not accept online orders, reservations, purchases or payments, and must not offer delivery checkout or an online shopping cart. Do not add “Buy Now”, “Add to Cart”, “Checkout” or similar e-commerce functionality.
>
> **Information accuracy:** Business information below was gathered from the existing Thilaka Ayurveda website and earlier public-page review. Treat business claims as claims made by the business, not independently verified facts. Items marked **Confirm before publishing** must be checked with the owner. Product data, prices, ingredients, directions, certifications and health claims must never be invented.

## 3. Scope and Business Rules

### 3.1 Included in scope

- Public-facing informational website.
- Dynamic product catalogue.
- Product categories and category pages.
- Product detail pages.
- Product search and filters.
- Contact details and shop location.
- Contact/enquiry form.
- Optional product enquiry link that opens a contact method or enquiry form; this is for questions only and is not an order.
- Secure backend/admin dashboard.
- Backend management of products, categories, pages and other website content.
- Management of opening hours, contact details and social links.
- Management of FAQs, articles/news, testimonials and image galleries, if the business chooses to publish them.
- SEO fields and basic website analytics integration.

### 3.2 Explicitly excluded — do not build online selling

The following are **not part of this project**:

- Shopping cart.
- Add to cart or buy now actions.
- Checkout process.
- Online card, bank, wallet or other payment processing.
- Customer purchase accounts or order history.
- Online orders or order placement.
- Product reservation or stock holding.
- Delivery selection, delivery fees or courier integration.
- Order confirmation, fulfilment or shipment tracking.
- Online returns/refunds workflow.
- Any interface that suggests a customer can complete a purchase through the website.

Do not label contact messages as orders. Do not describe a product enquiry as a purchase request. A customer who wants to buy must be directed to visit the shop.

### 3.3 Required customer-facing notice

Display a clear notice in suitable locations, especially on the Products page and product detail pages:

> **Please note:** This website is a product catalogue only. Online purchases and orders are not available. To purchase a product, please visit our shop at No. 38, Main Street, Ruwanwella. Contact us to ask about product information or current availability before visiting.

The wording may be refined by the business, but the meaning must remain clear.

## 8. Product Catalogue — Core Website Feature

The product catalogue is a central feature of the new website. It must be dynamic and managed through the backend. The initial information gathered from the existing website is mostly category-level; a real product list must be collected from Thilaka Ayurveda before individual catalogue entries are published.

### 8.1 Product catalogue page

The Products page should provide:

- Page title and introductory text.
- Product search by product name or relevant keyword.
- Product category browsing.
- Filters for category and product form; additional filters can be enabled if reliable product data exists.
- Sorting options such as alphabetical order and newest additions.
- Product cards with image, name, category and short description.
- Pagination or a load-more option for large catalogues.
- Clear empty-state text when no products match a search or filter.
- A visible notice explaining that the website is a catalogue only and purchases must be made at the physical shop.
- A contact link for product questions or availability enquiries.

The catalogue should not display checkout, cart, order, reservation or online payment controls.

### 8.2 Individual product detail page

Each published product should have its own detail page with fields managed from the backend. Use only fields for which the business can provide verified information.

Recommended fields:

- Product name.
- URL-friendly page address / slug.
- Main product photograph.
- Additional product photographs or packaging gallery.
- Product category.
- Product form (for example, Churna, Vati / Gutika, medicated oil, Arishta / Asava or raw herb).
- Short summary.
- Full description.
- Ingredients, where verified.
- Pack size or quantity, where applicable.
- Manufacturer or supplier information, where applicable.
- Product registration or certification information, where applicable and verified.
- Directions supplied by the manufacturer or qualified professional, where appropriate.
- Warnings, contraindications and storage instructions from verified sources.
- Optional displayed price, controlled by a backend setting. A price shown is informational only and does not allow online purchase.
- Product availability label, if the business can maintain it accurately.
- Related products or related categories.
- SEO title and meta description.
- Publish / draft status.
- Featured-product status and display order.

**Availability wording:** Prefer “Contact the shop to confirm availability” unless staff can reliably keep product availability updated. Do not imply live inventory tracking unless a real inventory system is implemented and maintained.

### 8.3 Product availability statuses

If the business wants to show an availability indicator, the backend may provide these manually maintained statuses:

- **Contact shop to confirm** — default status; availability has not been confirmed online.
- **Generally available** — the shop currently expects the item to be available, but customers should confirm before visiting.
- **Temporarily unavailable** — the shop has marked the item as temporarily unavailable.

These statuses are informational only. They must not enable reservations or stock holding. The wording should make clear that availability can change.

## 9. Backend / Admin Dashboard — Content Management

The website must include a secure backend/admin dashboard. Authorized staff should be able to manage routine website content without editing source code or asking a developer for every change.

### 9.1 Dashboard overview

The dashboard home page should provide a clear overview and convenient shortcuts, such as:

- Total published products.
- Draft products.
- Total product categories.
- Recent contact enquiries.
- Unread / unhandled enquiries.
- Recently updated pages or products.
- Quick actions to add a product, category, article or FAQ.

Do not show sales, order, revenue, payment or delivery metrics because the website does not sell products online.

### 9.2 Product management

Administrators with the relevant permission should be able to:

- Create a new product.
- Edit product information.
- Upload, replace, reorder and remove product images.
- Assign one or more categories if the business needs this.
- Assign a product form.
- Add short and full descriptions.
- Add verified ingredient and pack-size information.
- Add optional price information or hide prices globally / per product.
- Set or update the informational availability label.
- Mark a product as featured.
- Add related products.
- Set SEO title, meta description and URL slug.
- Save as draft, preview, publish, unpublish or archive a product.
- Search, filter and sort products in the dashboard.
- Duplicate an existing product as a starting point for a new entry.
- View the date and administrator responsible for the latest update, where audit information is enabled.

Deleting a product should be restricted or require confirmation. Where practical, archiving should be available to preserve records and avoid broken links.

### 9.3 Product category management

Administrators should be able to:

- Add, edit and delete categories.
- Set category name, description, image and optional icon.
- Set a category's parent category if nested categories are required.
- Set display order.
- Publish, hide or archive a category.
- Assign products to categories and see the number of linked products.
- Set category SEO fields.

A category should not appear on the public website when it is unpublished or hidden, unless the administrator intentionally enables it.

### 9.4 Product form management

Allow administrators to manage reusable product forms such as Churna, Vati / Gutika, Medicated Oils, Arishta / Asava and Raw Herbs. Forms can be added, renamed, reordered or disabled from the backend.

### 9.5 Page and section management

Administrators should be able to manage the content of the main public pages:

- Home.
- About Us.
- Services.
- Quality & Ingredients.
- Contact Us.
- Other approved informational pages.

For each page, the backend should support:

- Page title and URL slug.
- Main heading and body content.
- Images and image captions.
- Reusable content sections.
- Call-to-action text and destination.
- Page visibility / publish status.
- SEO title and meta description.
- Preview before publication.

### 9.6 Homepage section management

The administrator should be able to:

- Edit hero heading, description, image and buttons.
- Select which product categories appear on the homepage.
- Select featured products.
- Edit business introduction and heritage sections.
- Edit the visit-the-shop section.
- Enable or disable optional sections.
- Change section display order.

### 9.7 Banner and announcement management

Allow staff to create, edit, schedule if supported, show or hide website announcements, such as:

- Temporary opening-hour changes.
- Public holiday notices.
- Shop announcements.
- New product-category announcements.

Announcements must not be used to advertise unsupported medical claims.

### 9.8 Contact information and opening-hours management

All business contact details should be editable from one backend settings area:

- Business display name.
- Address.
- Primary telephone number.
- Additional telephone numbers, if provided.
- Email address.
- Map location / directions URL.
- Opening days and opening/closing times.
- Closed days.
- Special holiday hours or temporary closure notice.
- Social media URLs.
- Footer contact text.

A change to the central contact settings should update the relevant website sections consistently, rather than requiring staff to edit several pages separately.

### 9.9 Contact enquiry management

The website should provide a general contact/enquiry form. Submitted messages should be available in the backend.

Administrators should be able to:

- View enquiries in a list.
- Open and read an enquiry.
- See submission date and contact details supplied by the visitor.
- Mark an enquiry as unread, read, in progress or resolved.
- Add internal notes, if required.
- Search or filter enquiries.
- Delete or archive enquiries according to the site's data-retention policy.
- Receive an email notification when a new enquiry arrives, if outbound email is configured.

The form may include an enquiry type such as General Question, Product Information or Product Availability. If the visitor chooses a product enquiry, they may select a product or have the product name pre-filled from a product page.

**Important:** An enquiry is only a request for information. It must not create an order, reserve stock, promise availability or initiate payment or delivery.

### 9.10 FAQs management

Administrators should be able to add, edit, reorder, publish, hide and delete frequently asked questions. Suggested topics include:

- Where is the shop located?
- What are the opening hours?
- Can I purchase products through the website?
- How can I check whether a product is available?
- What should I do if I need information about a product?

The answer to online purchases must clearly state that customers need to visit the physical shop to buy products.

### 9.11 Articles / knowledge-centre management

If the business chooses to publish educational content, the backend should support:

- Article title.
- Article summary.
- Main image.
- Article body.
- Author or reviewer name, if approved.
- Publication date.
- Category or tags.
- Draft / published status.
- SEO title and meta description.

Health-related articles must be reviewed by an appropriately qualified person before publication. The site should not automatically present general articles as diagnosis or personal medical advice.

### 9.12 Testimonials and reviews management

If the business chooses to display customer testimonials, administrators should be able to add, edit, approve, hide and remove them. Obtain appropriate customer consent before publication. Do not invent reviews, ratings or customer identities.

### 9.13 Gallery management

Administrators should be able to upload, caption, reorder, publish and remove photographs for:

- Shop exterior and interior.
- Products and packaging.
- Herbal ingredients.
- Approved business history photographs.
- Other genuine business-related images.

### 9.14 Footer and navigation management

The backend should allow authorized staff to update:

- Footer description.
- Contact details shown in the footer.
- Quick links.
- Social media links.
- Copyright text / year.
- Main navigation labels and visibility, where appropriate.

### 9.15 SEO and website settings

The backend should support:

- Website title and default meta description.
- Page-specific SEO title and description.
- Product-specific SEO title and description.
- Category-specific SEO title and description.
- Social sharing image where supported.
- Favicon / logo settings.
- Canonical URL settings where needed.
- Index / no-index controls for draft or private pages.
- Contact details and local business information.

### 9.16 Media library

Provide a shared media library so administrators can:

- Upload images.
- Preview images.
- Search or filter images.
- Add alternative text for accessibility.
- Add image titles or captions.
- Replace an image used on a page or product.
- Reuse an existing image where appropriate.
- Remove unused images safely.

The system should optimize image sizes for web display while preserving adequate quality.

### 9.17 Admin users and permissions

Provide secure administrator accounts. Suggested roles:

- **Super Admin:** Full access, including admin-user and permission management and all website settings.
- **Content Manager:** Manage pages, homepage sections, banners, FAQs, articles, gallery and testimonials.
- **Catalogue Manager:** Manage products, categories, product forms, images and catalogue content.
- **Enquiry Manager:** View and manage contact enquiries.

The business may begin with one Super Admin and add other accounts as necessary. Permissions should be configurable so users only access the functions they need.

### 9.18 General admin requirements

- Secure login and logout.
- Password reset process.
- Role-based permissions.
- Confirmation before destructive actions.
- Form validation and clear success/error messages.
- Draft and published content states.
- Preview content before publishing.
- Record creation and last-update timestamps.
- Basic activity/audit history for important administrative actions, where practical.
- Session timeout and sensible account security controls.
